Make motion graphics in After Effects with AI

By Jethin · 1 October 2026 · 2 min read

After Effects can be scripted with ExtendScript, which can create compositions, add layers, set keyframes and fill the render queue. Building a lower third or a title sequence by script is fast, but writing the script is fiddly. With Homebox, the AI writes it, After Effects runs it, and you approve each step.

One-time setup

  1. Allow scripts to write filesIn After Effects choose Edit → Preferences → Scripting & Expressions and tick Allow Scripts to Write Files and Access Network. Homebox reads each step's result back through a small file, so this setting is required.
  2. Turn on After Effects in HomeboxGo to Apps → After Effects, switch on Let AI control After Effects and click Test connection while After Effects is open.

Build a title animation

Start a new chat, click the Homebox button and ask:

In After Effects, create a 1920x1080, 30 fps, 8-second comp
called "Intro". Add a dark background solid, then a text layer
"Summer Showcase" that slides in from the left over the first
second with an ease, holds, and fades out over the last second.
Add a smaller subtitle that appears 0.5 s after the title.
Report the layer names when done.

Each approved step is wrapped in an undo group, so Ctrl+Z in After Effects removes the whole step. Ask for one element per step, such as the comp, then the title, then the animation, so you can preview as you go.

Useful follow-ups

  • "Make the slide-in use a stronger ease out." The AI adjusts keyframe interpolation.
  • "Add a 2-pixel accent line under the title that grows from 0 to full width." A shape layer with an animated trim path or scale.
  • "Duplicate the comp for a 1080x1920 vertical version and re-center everything."
  • "Add the comp to the render queue with the default settings, output to the sandbox folder."

Tips

  • Indexes start at 1. In ExtendScript, app.project.item(1) is the first item. If the AI picks the wrong comp, ask it to find comps by name instead.
  • Expressions are text. The AI can add expressions such as wiggle(2, 10) to properties. Ask for expressions when you want motion that adapts to changes.
  • Save often. Undo covers each step, but saving the project (or asking the AI to save a copy into the sandbox) protects longer sessions.

Troubleshooting

  • "did not answer in time": the preference in step 1 is off, or a dialog is open in After Effects.
  • "not found in Program Files": After Effects is installed in an unusual place. Reinstall it to the default location.

More prompts to try

  • "Make a lower third with my name and role that animates in from the bottom and out after four seconds."
  • "Create a 15-second countdown comp from 10 to 0 with a pulse on each number."
  • "Add a vignette and a slow push-in (scale 100% to 110%) to the Intro comp."
  • "List every comp in the project with its size, frame rate and duration."
