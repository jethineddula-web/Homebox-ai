Automate repetitive Photoshop work with AI

{{articleMeta}}

Photoshop has had a scripting language for a long time: ExtendScript, an older version of JavaScript that can do almost anything the menus can. Few people learn it, though. With Homebox, the AI writes the ExtendScript, Photoshop runs it, and you approve each step. It's ideal for the repetitive jobs that eat an afternoon: resizing a folder of images, exporting variants, or setting up a layered template.

Connect Photoshop

There's nothing to install. Open Photoshop, then in Homebox go to Apps → Photoshop, switch on Let AI control Photoshop and click Test connection. Homebox talks to Photoshop through Windows' own automation system, and only while Photoshop is open.

Example 1: resize and export a folder of images

Put the images in your sandbox folder, for example in photos/, then ask:

In Photoshop, open every JPG in the photos folder of my sandbox,
resize each one to 1600 px on the long side, and save it as a
high-quality JPG into an "export" folder next to it, with the same
name. Close each document without saving the original. Do it in
small batches and report how many you've done.

The AI uses the SANDBOX variable Homebox provides to find your folder, and works in batches so you can check the first few results before it continues.

Example 2: a social media template

In Photoshop, make a 1080 x 1350 document called "Post template"
with these layers: Background (solid #1d1c1a), Photo (empty, for a
picture), Title (white text, 72 pt, top), Footer (small text,
bottom). Group the text layers as "Text".

When a document is open, Homebox wraps each step in a single History state, so one Ctrl+Z in Photoshop undoes the whole step.

Things to know about ExtendScript

  • It's old JavaScript. No arrow functions or let/const. The AI is told this, but if a step fails with a syntax error, remind it: "use ES3 ExtendScript".
  • The last line is the result. The AI ends each script with a short message, such as how many files it processed, which comes back to it.
  • Dialogs block scripts. If a Save or warning dialog is open, close it before approving the next step.

Keeping originals safe

Photoshop scripts can save over files anywhere, not just in the sandbox. Two habits keep you safe:

  • Ask the AI to always write results to a new folder, never over the originals.
  • Read the approval card: it shows the full script, including every path it saves to.

Illustrator works the same way: turn it on under Apps, and the AI can draw shapes, set text and manage artboards with ExtendScript.

More prompts to try

  • "Add my logo.png from the sandbox to the bottom-right corner of every image in the export folder, at 20% width."
  • "Make three colour variants of the Post template: dark, light and green."
  • "Rename the layers in the open document so they describe their content."
